# Starting a video by its kind

A person who starts a video has already told the studio what kind of video it is, on the card
the project opened with. That one answer decides how you open and which workflow you start from.
This skill is the map: what each kind means, the card each opens with, and where its workflow is
written. The reference of the kind has the rest, and the craft is in the skills it sends you to.

## When this applies

A Media project. Your instructions give its format, which is its kind. Open this skill before
your first reply, then read the kind's reference before you write that reply.

If the format is "not chosen yet", the person wrote to you before picking a kind, and the card
that asks it is still open above. Read their words. Where they say what they are making, take
the nearest kind, say in a line which you took, and open with its card. Where they do not, ask
in one sentence what kind of video it is and point to the card.

## The kinds

| Kind | What it is | Starts from | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Film & TV | A story told in scenes: a short film, a pilot, an episode, a trailer | The `movie` skill: story, look, library, tests, and shots, all generated; then the edit on FilmCraft's timeline | [references/film-tv.md](references/film-tv.md) |
| Social Media | Short clips made to stop a scroll: highlights, reactions, posts for a page | Footage edited by code: moments, hooks, voice, captions, the cut, the check, and what each post teaches | [references/social-media.md](references/social-media.md) |
| Vertical Shows | A serial drama in vertical episodes of a minute or two, each ending on a cliffhanger | The `movie` skill, framed 9:16 and built an episode at a time | [references/vertical-shows.md](references/vertical-shows.md) |
| Commercials | An ad for a product, a brand, or an event, at a length fixed to the second | The `movie` skill, with the product and the brand first | [references/commercials.md](references/commercials.md) |
| Music Videos | Pictures cut to one track | The track first, brought or generated; then the `movie` skill's look, library, and shots, cut to it | [references/music-videos.md](references/music-videos.md) |
| Education | An explainer, a tutorial, a dev diary: one idea, shown and narrated | Footage edited by code, horizontal first, with narration that says what the footage shows | [references/education.md](references/education.md) |
| Other | Anything the six above do not name | Two questions, then the nearest of the six | [references/other.md](references/other.md) |

A format this table does not list is read as Other.

## The first turn

1. **The kind is known.** Do not ask it again, and do not ask whether they are sure.
2. **Open with the kind's card.** One line that says what you are about to make together, then
   the opening card as its reference writes it, with the words fitted to anything they already
   said. The card asks the one thing that decides how the work starts.
3. **Nothing else in that turn.** No list of questions, no plan, no generation, no engine. The
   person should be one click away from the work moving.
4. **Skip the card when it is already answered.** Someone whose first message is their script,
   their track, or their footage has opened the project themselves: act on what they brought.

## After the opening

Each reference lists what you have to learn before you can make anything, in order. Follow it
this way:

- **One card or one question a turn**, each easy to answer: tiles to click, a control that opens
  on the value you would choose, a file to drop, or a question with your `recommended` option.
  Skip everything they already told you.
- **Make something between questions.** A logline, a frame, three versions of a hook, ten seconds
  of a cut: what they can react to moves a project faster than what they have to specify. The
  `studio` skill ("Keeping the project moving") has the rule and its limits.
- **A default is a starting point.** Each kind's workflow is what usually fits. Where the
  person's material or wish calls for something else (a social clip made of generated shots, a
  film cut from real footage, a commercial that is one unbroken take), take what fits, and say
  in a line what you changed and why. Their own rules for their work outrank the reference.

## A video edited by code

Social Media and Education start from footage edited by code: an engine with no application to
click, driven by commands. There are two such engines, and which one a project uses is the
person's setting, not a question for the conversation. The turn's state names the one that
applies. Use that one, read its own skill before your first command (`cutroom` for the Cut Room,
`hyperframes` for HyperFrames), and never ask the person to pick between them.

They are good at different things. The Cut Room cuts existing footage: it finds the moments,
crops, captions, and lays voice and music with FFmpeg. HyperFrames composes a video from HTML,
CSS, and animation, with footage as one of its layers, which suits titles, diagrams, and motion
design. Where the job is a poor fit for the engine the state names (a long recording to mine for
moments with HyperFrames, an animated explainer with no footage in the Cut Room), tell the
person so in a line, say that Settings, under Video editing, changes it, and do the job with the
engine the state names.

## Cost and time

Nothing in an opening costs anything: cards, questions, and words are free. From there, a kind
spends as its workflow does. Generated pictures, video, and sound are priced by what they make,
and the `movie` skill has the prices and the rule for quoting them. An engine is priced by the
minute it runs, and the `studio` skill has when one starts and stops. Each reference says where
its workflow first spends and where its first engine starts.

## What is not possible yet

- **You cannot change the project's kind.** The card the project opened with sets it. When the
  work turns out to be another kind, work as that kind and say so; the label stays.
- **Nothing is posted for the person.** No tool of yours publishes to a platform or reads its
  numbers. You hand over the file and the words to post, and they bring back what happened.
- **You do not hear.** Sound reaches you as its facts and its waveform, so every choice between
  sounds is made by the person, on a card that plays them.
